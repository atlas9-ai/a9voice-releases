    Usage and cost

    What your notes, dictation and Ask cost at your own keys' rates, how the estimate is made, and how to change the prices it uses.

    Last updated for A9 Voice 1.0.46

    A9 Voice is free. The speech and language services you connect bill you directly, with your own keys. Settings › Usage & cost estimates that bill. It has four tabs: Overview, Savings, History and Prices. The estimate is built from what A9 Voice sent and a price table, so your provider's own bill is always the final word.

    What is counted#

    Every time something is sent to a service you pay for, A9 Voice writes it down, with the model and how it was sent:

    • A meeting's audio, live. With the live transcript on and a cloud live service chosen (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Soniox or Modulate), each side of the call (you, and the other people) is streamed while the Live window is open. That is billed at the provider's live rate, for each side, for as long as it streams: only the seconds that were really sent are counted, and a meeting nobody watched has none. Add-ons the service bills on top (speaker labels on the other people's side, your Dictionary's words) are written down with the row, so each has its own price.
    • A meeting's audio, after the call. When the meeting ends, each side is uploaded again to the Meeting notes model for the final transcript, at its after-the-call rate. With Settings › Transcript & summary › Send only speech on (it is off until you turn it on), long quiet stretches of each side are left out, so you pay for talking and not for long silences; the row says how many seconds were sent and how long the side was. A side that was silent is not uploaded and costs nothing.
    • Dictation. Each clip you dictate, at the after-the-call rate of the dictation model that answered. Dictation on this PC costs nothing.
    • Summaries, Ask and Enhance. Each call to a language model, by the tokens the provider counted. OpenRouter also says what each call cost, and that number is used instead of the table.

    A meeting is written in two passes, and each is billed on its own: the live transcript during the call, then the transcript your notes are made from after it (see Two passes). A meeting you watched is billed twice for the part you watched: once live, once after the call. The live part runs only while the window is open (Settings › Notes › Live window › Live transcript), and it comes from whoever you pick under Live transcript by. Set it to Off if you only want the final notes. Your Dictionary's words go to the after-the-call pass always, and to the live transcript only if you turn on Use Dictionary words in the live transcript (a cloud stream bills them as an add-on).

    Models on this PC (Parakeet, Whisper) are always $0.00.

    Two passes#

    A meeting is written in two passes. During the call, the live transcript is a draft, written by whoever you pick under Settings › Notes › Live window › Live transcript by: a model on this PC (free) or a live service. After the call, your Meeting notes engine writes the transcript your notes are made from. They are two jobs with their own model, their own price and their own setting, so the Live window can say Whisper Large v3 Turbo on your graphics card while your notes come from Soniox. The app says it in one sentence wherever the two meet: During the call: Whisper Large v3 Turbo · graphics card writes the live transcript, a draft. After the call: Soniox STT v6 writes the transcript your notes are made from. With the live transcript off, the first half says so.

    The four jobs are named the same everywhere: Dictation, Live transcript (during the call), Meeting notes (after the call) and AI (summaries and Ask). They are the four chips on top of Settings, in that order, and the four tiles on the Savings tab. The Live transcript chip shows who is writing the live draft now (Whisper Large v3 Turbo · graphics card, Soniox · STT v6 Realtime, or Off), and its menu is the same list as Live transcript by, so a pick there is the same pick, with the same questions (Modulate still asks once about its data use).

    Overview#

    Pick a period at the top right: this month (the default), last month, 90 days or All time. Everything on the tab follows it.

    • The gauge shows what you spent against an optional monthly budget. The pale arc is where the month ends if you keep this pace. With no budget the arc scales to that projection and the card says Set a budget. Over budget it turns amber and says so in words.
    • Cost per meeting hour is what a meeting costs per hour of recording, transcript and summary together. Set a target and the bar shows it, and says how many cents you are over it.
    • Meetings shows the count, the hours, and a small line of daily spend. Imported notes aren't counted: nothing was billed for them.
    • Dictation shows the cost, the count, the minutes and the engine. When dictation is free, it says about how much the same minutes would have cost on the cloud model you use for Meeting notes.
    • Summaries & Ask shows their cost, their share of everything you spend, and which providers it went to.
    • Daily spend has a bar for every day, split by provider. Point at a bar, or press the left and right arrow keys once the chart has focus, to read that day. A dashed line is the average per day.
    • By provider is the same money by provider, with one sentence about where most of it goes.
    • Most expensive lists the three notes that cost the most. Click one to open it.

    Savings#

    The Savings tab asks one question: are you on the right models for what you do? It reads the last 30 days of your own usage.

    • A tile for each job, in the same order and with the same names as the model chips at the top: Dictation, Live transcript (during the call), Meeting notes (after the call) and AI (summaries and Ask). Good fit means nothing comparable is both more than a quarter cheaper and at least $1 a month cheaper at your pace. Otherwise it says Could save and how much a month. Only a Could save counts toward the number on the tab and the headline. Dictation on this PC says Already free. A service that may train on your audio is judged like any other, with its amber May train on your audio beside it (see below).
    • How the AI card decides. It takes the tokens your summaries and Ask used in the last 30 days and prices them on the model you use today, then on each other model you have a key for, so money you spent on a model you have since left is never counted as a saving. It only suggests a model of the same tier or better: Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-4.1 mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash and GPT-OSS 120B are Standard; GPT-OSS 20B, GPT-4.1 nano, GPT-4o mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite are Basic. A model A9 Voice has no tier for (OpenRouter's Any model, a custom API) is never suggested and never compared against, and a model with no price says so. When it says Could save it names the model (Switch to GPT-4.1 mini to save $1.40/mo) with a Switch button. A call whose prompt is over 100,000 tokens is priced at the model's long-prompt rate: Claude Haiku 5.5 charges five times its usual price for those.
    • The two passes each have a tile. Live transcript · during the call says You use: the source writing the draft, then $0 · stays on this PC or what it costs per meeting hour and a month at your pace; when a model on your graphics card keeps up, its amber line says Could save $X/mo with your graphics card and jumps to the engine's card, which has the Switch. Meeting notes · after the call says what the after-call pass costs. The two tiles' months always add up to the engine card's month marked Current; with the live transcript off the Live tile says Off and bills nothing.
    • The tile's hourly figure is the engine's own. Each tile says what its pass costs per meeting hour and a month at your pace, and the two always agree: the hourly figure times your hours is the monthly one. When the last 30 days were paid to other engines or settings, one quiet line under it says so (Last 30 days: $0.85 per meeting hour, mostly AssemblyAI); it is never mixed into the first.
    • Each tile names its saving. It says You use: and the model you use now, and when something is cheaper the amber line says Could save $24.64/mo with Deepgram Nova-3: the engine the saving is with, never the one you already use. On the Meeting notes tile the line jumps to that engine's card, and its figure is the Current card's month minus that card's month.
    • A price check (amber) appears when a provider you use was billed in a way the price table has no rate for, or when a price you typed is far from the shipped one. It links to Prices.
    • The map is Meeting notes engines: cost vs. what they do. Its rows are what an engine can do on its own, taken from what each one supports and never from your settings: Live and after the call, speakers labeled (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Soniox, Modulate), After the call, speakers labeled (ElevenLabs), After the call, speakers told apart on this PC (Groq, OpenAI and servers you add) and On this PC, free. The engine that does the most is the top row, where the green sweet spot sits, and only rows that hold an engine are drawn. Where a dot sits left to right is what a meeting hour costs with your settings as they are now (left is cheaper), the same figure as its card, and the line under the map's title says which live source those prices assume (Live transcript: this PC, free). Your engine is ringed. The rows do not move when you change your live transcript, so two engines never look the same just because your live draft comes from one place. The dollar labels under the map are placed on the same scale as the dots. Sorting the cards by price gives the map's left-to-right order.
    • A card for each option says what that engine does: what it would cost a month at your pace (priced with your own live setting, and a quiet line under the card says what that includes, such as Includes your live transcript: $0, this PC), what you would save a month and a year, After the call (its price per meeting hour), Speakers (how well it tells people apart; an engine with no labels of its own reads Told apart on this PC, less reliable on busy calls once the speaker files are installed, and offers their download until then), Live transcript (Can write it too: instant, +$0.92 per meeting hour with a Use for live link that sets it as your live source after a confirmation, or After the call only), and its accuracy. Accuracy says Not measured yet until you run a comparison. The current engine's card carries the two-pass sentence above.
    • Your live transcript. Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Soniox and Modulate stream a live transcript from the cloud (billed by how long the stream stays connected). The monthly figure uses the live hours really sent, so the default (stream only while the window is open) shows up as less to pay right away. If you chose a live service other than the one that writes your notes (Settings › Notes › Live window › Live transcript by), the confirmation when you switch says it stays on, billed separately. A live transcript written by a model on this PC, including one on your graphics card, costs nothing. When a model on your card keeps up with a live meeting, the card for the engine you use also offers to move the live transcript there, with what its month would come to without the cloud live stream.
    • Services that may train on your audio are compared like the others, with their warning. Modulate says it may use the audio you send unless you opt out. Savings gives it the same verdicts as every other engine: Best value, Cheapest cloud, Could save and Good fit. Wherever a verdict names such a service (the tile's Could save $X/mo with Modulate Velma Multilingual, a badge, the Switch confirmation) the amber phrase May train on your audio sits beside it, and its card keeps the amber note from its window with a link. When it is the engine you use, the tile gives it its verdict with the same phrase beside it. Compare on a meeting works against it and shows the same note before anything is sent. Its rows are priced like any other: Velma Multilingual $0.03 an hour after the call and $0.06 live, Velma English Fast $0.025 and $0.05, with custom terms (your Dictionary on Velma Multilingual) a row of their own at $0.02 more. A live hour is billed by the audio sent, for each side.
    • Hide services that may train on your audio. A switch above the cards, off by default. On, such services leave the cards, the map and every verdict on this tab (the rules run as if they were not in the list), except the one you use, which stays with its warning. It is remembered, and it only filters Savings: the pickers still list every service with its amber line.
    • Soniox is billed by tokens. It lists about $0.10 an hour after the call and $0.12 live, with speaker labels and language detection included, and A9 Voice prices the audio it sent at those figures: each row says List price (about: billed by tokens), and a side with little speech costs a little less than it shows. Its live stream is billed for as long as it stays connected.
    • Every add-on is counted. AssemblyAI and Deepgram bill speaker labels and your Dictionary's words (key terms) on top of the plain rate, after the call and live. The card for the engine you use is made from what it really billed you in the last 30 days (with its live stream left out once your live transcript is not a cloud one): those meetings' priced rows, live and after the call together, at your pace for a month. The other cards come from the price table with the add-ons your settings would turn on for that engine. Only meetings written by the model you use count: a different model of the same service (Universal-2 beside Universal-3.5 Pro) is priced on its own. With less than two meeting hours on your engine's model, its card is made from the table too.
    • The basis is written under the cards: At your pace over the last 30 days: 48 meeting hours. Last 7 days on AssemblyAI: $16.30. The second sentence appears when your engine has rows in the last 7 days. When the live transcript is on this PC it says Live transcript on this PC since Nov 19: not counted., because the stream your older meetings paid for is no longer part of what you pay.
    • Silence is not sent. Once meetings have been sent with Send only speech, a line says how much it saved: Silence not sent: 31 of 76 audio hours in the last 30 days, about $9.40 at AssemblyAI's rate. Until there are about two meeting hours of them, the older meetings are priced as they were billed and a quiet line says the figures drop as new meetings come in; after that the month at your pace uses what will be billed from now on. While the setting is off nothing is scaled and these two lines are not shown; one offer line says about what it would save a month at your pace, with Try it on a meeting and Switch.
    • A nudge for "Always". If Live transcript is set to Always and the Live window was closed for most of the streamed time in the last 30 days, a line says how many hours and what they cost, with Switch to While open to change it in one click.

    Switch on a meeting engine card changes the Meeting notes engine after you confirm, and nothing else: Dictation, Summaries and Ask keep their models, and notes you already have stay as they are. With no key saved for that provider it takes you to Settings › Models to add one. Switch on the AI card changes only the AI chip, after you confirm, to a model you already have a key for.

    Compare on a meeting sends one recent meeting's audio to a second provider after the call and shows both transcripts' word counts, how many words differ, speaker counts, the time it took and its cost. It shows the cost before it sends anything, it counts in Usage & cost like any upload, and your note is not changed. The result fills in that engine's Accuracy and Source. A model on this PC can't be compared this way. With a cloud engine writing your notes, the dialog also offers This engine: whole recording vs. only the speech: the meeting's audio goes to that engine twice, whole and as only the speech, so it bills one extra upload (the cost is shown first). The result shows the word counts, how many words differ, the words that appear only in the whole run, the minutes each way and the money the cut saved on that meeting. It never changes the note. Use it on your own meetings before turning Send only speech on. The other side of the comparison is the transcript your note already has, from whichever engine wrote that meeting, which may not be the engine you use today: the window names it above the provider list (Compared against: AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro, or your existing transcript on an older note that didn't keep it), and that engine is left out of the list, because it can't be compared with itself.

    History#

    Every note and dictation, newest first. Today and Yesterday are open. The rest of the month is one group, Earlier in October, and every past month is one line with its total. Open one and it shows 25 rows at a time, with Newer and Older buttons.

    • A day's dictations are one row, for example Dictation · 53 clips.
    • A row shows the note's title, each provider it used and how (live, after the call, on this PC), its length and its cost. Click a note to open it.
    • All, Meetings, Dictation and Ask filter the rows. The search box looks in titles.
    • Export CSV saves every row that matches the filter and the search: date, kind, title, providers, mode, units and cost.
    • The calendar colors each day by what it cost; darker is more. The box under it gives the busiest day, the longest meeting and the averages.

    A row that says Not tracked is a note nothing was recorded for. That is every note imported from another app, and any note whose engine was never written down. It is not counted as $0.00. A stretch that mixes older notes with newer ones says so: older notes were written down before live and after-the-call audio were told apart, so they are priced at the after-the-call rate.

    No usage is deleted. Clearing your dictation history or deleting a note does not take its spending out of these pages.

    Prices#

    Every estimate is units times the price of that model, in that mode.

    1. Ships with the app. Each provider's public list price, checked on the date shown on the page. A new version of A9 Voice brings new defaults.
    2. You can override any price. Got a discount or a credit? Click a price and type yours. Press Enter to keep it, Esc to cancel; clear the box to go back to the shipped number. An edited price is marked Edited and stays through every update. A price you change re-prices everything in the period, old rows too.
    3. Real cost where offered. OpenRouter reports what each call cost. A9 Voice uses that number, and its row says Real cost per call.

    Speech has two rates: After the call and Live, per audio hour. Language models have Input and Output, per 1 million tokens. A dash means that model has no rate for that mode, and anything billed that way shows no price and counts as $0.00 until you set one.

    Only the providers you use show at first. Show unused providers lists the rest. A new live rate carries a new mark the first time you open the tab. Reset to defaults removes every price you typed, after a confirmation.

    If something looks wrong#

    • The total is lower than your provider's bill. Check Live in Prices: a rate that is blank is counted as $0.00. Check for a promotion or a discount your account has; type that rate and the page follows it.
    • A note says Not tracked. It was imported, or it was recorded before A9 Voice kept usage. Nothing is hidden: it simply has no numbers.
    • Costs went up after an update. Meetings recorded before this version were priced after the call only. The live stream is now counted too, which is what Deepgram bills.
